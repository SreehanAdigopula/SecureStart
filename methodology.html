How scoring works

SecureStart turns self-reported security habits into a simple readiness gap score. This page shows the full method, evidence base, and limits.

Scoring model 2.0 Reviewed September 22, 2026
The important part

A higher score means more reported gaps. The score is an educational prioritization tool, not a breach prediction, certification, compliance grade, or professional audit.

What the score measures

The assessment checks six areas: account security, access control, data protection, devices and updates, website and domain safety, and incident readiness. The questions focus on baseline practices that small organizations can understand and act on.

SecureStart measures reported readiness gaps. It does not measure every technical control, inspect real settings, or calculate the probability or financial impact of a cyber incident.

The formula

1. Answer points

  • 0 points: the practice is in place.
  • 1 point: the practice is mostly or partly in place.
  • 2 points: the practice is inconsistent or the user is not sure.
  • 3 points: the practice is absent or creates a clear gap.

2. Category scores

Each category is normalized so categories with more questions do not automatically control the result.

Category score = (category gap points / category maximum points) x 100

3. Overall score

The applicable category scores are averaged equally and rounded to the nearest whole number.

Overall score = sum of applicable category scores / number of applicable categories

Website and domain safety is excluded when the profile says the organization has no website. No other profile answer secretly changes a category's numeric weight.

Score bands

ScoreBandMeaning
0 to 24Low gapsFew baseline gaps were reported. Continue routine reviews and maintenance.
25 to 49Moderate gapsSeveral preventable gaps need attention and a practical improvement plan.
50 to 74High gapsMajor gaps could make prevention, response, or recovery difficult.
75 to 100Urgent gapsWidespread gaps call for prompt action, starting with accounts, updates, backups, and recovery.

These bands are a SecureStart heuristic for prioritizing basic work. NIST, CISA, FTC, and OWASP do not publish or endorse these numeric cutoffs.

Worked example

Suppose an organization reports these category results:

  • Account Security: 3 of 11 gap points = 27
  • Access Control: 3 of 9 gap points = 33
  • Data Protection: 4 of 12 gap points = 33
  • Devices and Updates: 3 of 9 gap points = 33
  • Website and Domain Safety: 2 of 9 gap points = 22
  • Incident Readiness: 5 of 9 gap points = 56
(27 + 33 + 33 + 33 + 22 + 56) / 6 = 34

The final score is 34, which falls in the Moderate gaps band.

What profile information affects

  • Website status: decides whether website and domain questions are included.
  • Data handling: adds context and may prioritize a data-handling recommendation.
  • Organization size: may prioritize access-review guidance for larger groups.
  • Organization type and label: appear in the report but do not alter the score.

Profile details can personalize explanations and recommendations. They do not add hidden numeric multipliers.

Limits and responsible use

  • Results depend on the accuracy of the user's answers.
  • SecureStart does not inspect devices, accounts, websites, networks, files, or security settings.
  • A low score does not guarantee that an organization is secure or will avoid an incident.
  • A high score does not prove that a breach has occurred.
  • The assessment is not a legal, regulatory, insurance, or contractual compliance determination.
  • Organizations with sensitive systems, regulated data, or active incidents should seek qualified professional help.
